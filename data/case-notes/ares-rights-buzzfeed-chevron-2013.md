# Ares Rights — BuzzFeed & Chevron (Ecuador)

## 2013 — BuzzFeed (ARES-001)

Leaked documents on Ecuadorian intelligence purchases of surveillance equipment, published by BuzzFeed, were first hosted on Scribd and taken down after a DMCA notice from Ares Rights. BuzzFeed re-uploaded them to Dropbox; Ares Rights sent another notice with the same outcome. Per EFF (SRC-001), BuzzFeed counter-noticed and the files were reinstated. CPJ (SRC-040) reports that BuzzFeed put the documents back online by switching to a different U.S.-based hosting service and that the complaint was filed on behalf of clients including named officials of Ecuador's intelligence agency.

Motive category: political state censorship. Confidence: Confirmed (SRC-001; SRC-040).

## Late November 2013 — Chevron (ARES-002)

Chevron YouTube videos describing alleged fraud and corruption in the Ecuadorian judicial system were taken down after copyright claims filed by Ares Rights. EFF (SRC-001) reports the takedown; Free Beacon (SRC-041) and Techdirt (SRC-042) relay Chevron's own statement ("our videos on The Amazon Post are currently down"). In December 2013 Chevron said it was working to get the videos back up; no source documents their restoration.

Motive category: commercial reputation scrubbing (as coded; the videos criticised the Ecuadorian government, and EFF links Ares Rights' notices to Ecuadorian officials). Confidence: Unverified — removal rests on EFF and Chevron's statements; no Lumen record or Wayback snapshot has been obtained.
